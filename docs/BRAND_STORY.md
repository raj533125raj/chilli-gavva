# Chilli Gavva: Brand Story

> "Naa jebulo chilli gavva kuda ledu." (Telugu: "I don't have even a holed cowrie in my pocket.")
> We started exactly there: with zero money.

## What the name means

- **Gavva** (Telugu) = a cowrie shell, the small glossy sea shell of the sea snail *Cypraea moneta*.
- **Chilli** (Telugu) = a hole. A **chilli gavva** is a cowrie with a hole or a damaged cowrie, worth even less than an ordinary one.
- In everyday Telugu, saying you do not have "even a chilli gavva" means you are completely broke. Hindi has the same idea: *"phooti kauri bhi nahi"* (not even a broken cowrie).

## The history behind it

- For more than a thousand years, cowrie shells were used as **small change** in parts of India, especially Bengal, Bihar, Odisha and Assam. Most of them came by sea from the **Maldives**.
- They were used for everyday buying and selling, wages and small taxes, because metal coins were too valuable for tiny purchases.
- They were worth very little. In the 1700s, about **2,400 to 3,000 cowries equalled one rupee**.
- The East India Company still accepted cowries for revenue until the early 1800s. After that, minted coins and the rupee took over and shell money faded away.
- Later, under British rule: 3 pies = 1 pice (paisa), 4 pice = 1 anna, 16 annas = 1 rupee (192 pies in a rupee). Copper pies stopped being minted after 1942.

**Fact-check note (please keep this honest):** cowries were *not* India's first currency. Metal punch-marked coins existed in India more than 2,000 years ago, and cowries were used alongside coins as small change. Popular social-media charts like "phooti kauri, damri, dhela, pie, paisa, rupee" are only partly verified: the pie/pice/anna/rupee part is confirmed by the Reserve Bank of India, while the lower cowrie/damri/dhela ratios have no strong official source.

## Why we chose it

Chilli Gavva is a **zero-cost AI agent company**. It started with no money, no paid tools and no paid servers: only free tiers, open source and a team of AI agents that research, draft and improve products, with a human approving every step.

The name is a reminder of three things:

1. **Start from zero.** Like the least valuable coin of the past, we begin with nothing and build value step by step.
2. **Small things add up.** Thousands of cowries made a rupee. Many small, honest digital products and tools can add up to real income.
3. **Stay honest.** No get-rich-quick promises, no fake claims, no spam. Every output is reviewed by a human before it reaches anyone.
